How people who do this for a living think about it

Pick the genre first

Material family and palette come before geometry. A glossy metal lock, a toon lock and a halftone lock are three different projects built from the same mesh.

Light for mood, not visibility

Warm key, cool fill, a coloured rim from behind. Complementary temperature between key and fill is the cheapest way to make a render feel designed. Hard shadows say "illustration"; soft environment light says "product".

Restrain the palette

Four or five colours and one accent. In every preset the ground and background are nearly the same colour, so the horizon dissolves and the subject carries the frame.

Grade last

Tone mapping and exposure are the film stock and the grade. Switch to None and watch the highlights clip: that harshness is what an untreated three.js scene looks like.

Post is the signature

Bloom, grain, vignette, pixelation, dither and halftone are done on the finished image, so they work on any geometry. The more of them you stack, the more it is a style rather than a polish.

Model elsewhere, display here

Detailed work is usually built and baked in Blender, exported as glTF, and three.js just stages it. Procedural scenes like this one trade fidelity for total control and tiny file size. Lens, depth of field and real photography deserve their own page.

Scale assumptions are carried over from the padlock comparison: 12 mm of rim per symbol, so ring radius = symbols × 12 mm ÷ 2π. The seed-phrase ring holds 1,024 words (about 1.95 m radius); the password ring holds 95 characters (about 0.15 m). Rings tick to new positions when Idle motion is on. three.js r170 from a CDN, no build step.
